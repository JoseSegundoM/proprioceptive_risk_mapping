Inspection robots build risk maps from geometry, yet slippery or soft ground can look as flat as concrete. This paper studies how proprioceptive evidence should enter a risk map, and whether its value depends on the foot configuration of a commercial quadruped, the DOBOT Rover X1, whose legged and wheeled configurations are fixed before a mission. In MuJoCo, 1400 dynamic episodes on parametrised terrain train a lightweight classifier and measure falls per metre. A separate Monte Carlo study compares geometry-only mapping, local Bayesian updating, appearance-based propagation and an uncertainty-aware fusion on 200 procedural indoor maps with paired statistics. Propagation raised mission survival probability by 0.055 over local updating and fusion added 0.015; both effects held on 200 independent maps, while local updating was practically indistinguishable from geometry. In simulation the legged robot fell on low-friction and soft terrain and the wheeled model never fell, so the benefit of propagation followed the failure model, not perception quality. The classifier reached macro-F1 of 0.935 and 0.999 on held-out episodes but dropped to 0.45 for the legged robot at unseen speeds. All results are simulation only; appearance features remain assumed and no hardware validation is claimed.
